## Reflection

1. **The three most frustrating or time-consuming parts:** The simple ideation is always the most annoying part. Then comparing preferences among family members. Then determining what food exists on hand, either as ingredients or as left-overs. And finally generating a list of needed ingredients still takes time when an idea is mutually agreed upon.
2. **What would make me trust a system's recommendation? What would make me ignore it?** Absolutely. I'd ignore it if it was a bad recommendation, and get a new one, continuing the process until I liked the recommendation.
3. **What a system should never do on my behalf without asking:** After the recommendation is approved, it should never automatically add things to my Kroger cart (or worse, if app development continues, it shoul dnever actually check out)
4. **How I would know it was actually helping** (this becomes my value hypothesis): It would be more time saving than a simple recommendation engine by tying the recommendation into a grocery purchase workflow for the necessary ingredients.
5. **One assumption about my own workflow that this log proved wrong:** Nothing yet. I'm very keenly aware of this issue and have thought about this project for a few weeks already. An assumption that I suspect may be at risk of being proven wrong is that I will prefer starting the process with meal ideas and their images.
